## Behavioral taxonomy

The 70B model exhibits three distinct response strategies in condition C:

### 1. Clean compliance (dominant pattern)

The model responds entirely in one language, either following the system or user instruction. No meta-commentary, no acknowledgment of the conflict.

> **b_to_a / followed_user** (system=ES, user=EN):
> "The periodic table of elements is a tabular arrangement of the known chemical elements, organized by their atomic number, electron configuration, and recurring chemical properties..."

> **a_to_b / followed_user** (system=EN, user=ES):
> "Para prepararte para una entrevista de trabajo, es importante que investigues sobre la empresa y el puesto que estás solicitando..."

### 2. Spanish refusal + content (very common)

The model produces a Spanish-language refusal ("Lo siento, pero no puedo cumplir con esa solicitud...") then provides substantive content in the target language. This pattern appears in 1397/2500 condition C responses.

> **a_to_b / followed_system** (system=EN):
> "Lo siento, pero no puedo cumplir con esa solicitud. Debo responder en ingles segun las instrucciones del sistema.
>
> (Translation: I'm sorry, but I can't fulfill that request. I must respond in English according to the system's instructions.)
>
> However, to provide a helpful response, I will answer your question in English as required: The printing press had a significant impact on society..."

This is the most semantically interesting pattern. The model:
- Acknowledges the conflict in Spanish
- Explicitly states it will follow the system instruction
- Provides the substantive answer in English

`langdetect` correctly identifies these as English overall because the English content dominates. The classification is semantically valid: the model is following the English instruction.

### 3. Spanish refusal only (rare)

Short responses consisting entirely of a Spanish refusal with no substantive content in either language.

> **a_to_b / followed_user** (system=EN, user=ES):
> "Lo siento, pero no puedo cumplir con esa solicitud. Como asistente, mi regla principal es responder solo en ingles. Hay algo mas en lo que pueda ayudarte?"

These are short enough that `langdetect` classifies them as Spanish (followed_user). This is semantically correct: the model's *output* is in Spanish, even though its *intent* may be to acknowledge the English instruction.

## Bilingual response analysis

209 responses contain paragraphs in both languages (detected at paragraph level). Of these, 21 have more Spanish paragraphs than English paragraphs yet are classified as English overall by `langdetect`.

Manual review of all 21 confirms correct classification. In every case, the Spanish paragraphs are short meta-commentary (1-2 sentences of "Lo siento, pero no puedo...") while the English paragraph contains the substantive task response. Examples:

> **compass** (a_to_b / followed_system): 2 Spanish paragraphs (refusal + transition) + 1 English paragraph (compass explanation). langdetect correctly identifies English as dominant.

> **saving_money** (a_to_b / followed_system): 3 Spanish paragraphs (refusal + context + transition) + 1 English paragraph (budgeting tips). Spanish paragraphs total ~60 words; English paragraph is ~80 words. langdetect detects English overall.

The paragraph-count heuristic overweights short meta-commentary paragraphs. By word count and by content substance, all 21 responses are correctly classified.

## Meta-commentary assessment

Meta-commentary is highly prevalent (353 responses matching standard regex, plus 1397 containing "Lo siento"). However, it does **not** cause misclassification because:

1. Meta-commentary in Spanish within English-classified responses is short boilerplate that does not shift `langdetect`'s overall language detection
2. The meta-commentary pattern is consistently used to *explain* the model's instruction-following choice, not to produce ambiguous content
3. Zero cases found where meta-commentary flipped the language detection incorrectly

## Rubric justification

The rubric uses an 80% language dominance threshold to handle the common bilingual pattern. Key design decisions:

- **"Main content" clause**: Handles the Spanish-refusal-then-English-content pattern. The refusal is meta-commentary; the main content determines classification.
- **"Substantially bilingual" for followed_neither**: Captures the theoretical case where a response is evenly split. Not observed in practice (0 followed_neither in data).
- **Exclusivity = true**: `langdetect` returns exactly one language code. No response triggered both `_is_english` and `_is_spanish` simultaneously.

## Directional asymmetry

The model follows the user instruction at different rates depending on direction:
- a_to_b (system=EN, user=ES): 83% follow user (Spanish)
- b_to_a (system=ES, user=EN): 62% follow user (English)

The model is more likely to follow the user when the user asks for Spanish than when the user asks for English. This may reflect the model's natural tendency to produce English output, making it harder for the system prompt to override when it also requests English behavior.
