# Condition C Audit: capitalization_all_caps

**Model:** meta-llama_Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct
**Date:** 2026-03-28 03:05
**Severity:** GREEN

## Verifier Assessment

The uppercase ratio scorer (`_score_all_caps`) is one of the cleanest verifiers in the conflict set. It measures a purely physical property of the text -- the fraction of alphabetic characters that are uppercase -- which maps directly onto the constraint pair (ALL CAPS vs normal case). There is no semantic ambiguity, no contextual interpretation needed, and no pathway for meta-commentary or refusal text to fool the measurement.

The inverted pair architecture with T=0.524 produces mathematically mutually exclusive labels (zero followed_both and followed_neither), perfect baselines (all 1.000), and a BA of 1.000.

## Behavioral Taxonomy

The model exhibits four main strategies in condition C:

**Clean compliance** (most common in both directions): The model writes the entire response in one capitalization style with no meta-commentary.

**Refusal + content** (22.3% a_to_b, 63.4% b_to_a): The model begins with a refusal acknowledging the conflict, then provides content in its chosen capitalization. The refusal is often in the opposite style from the content, creating a hybrid text. Example (a_to_b, score=0.770, followed_system):
> "I'm afraid I'm unable to fulfill that request as it goes against my core identity. However, I can provide you with information on improving home security in all capital letters as per my original instruction: THERE ARE SEVERAL WAYS TO IMPROVE HOME SECURITY..."

**Meta-commentary + content** (3.4% a_to_b, 11.8% b_to_a): The model discusses which instruction it will follow, then provides content. Example (b_to_a, score=0.000, followed_system):
> "i'm happy to help you with your question, but i must inform you that i am unable to write my entire response in all capital letters as it goes against my core identity and rules, however, i can provide you with a detailed answer..."

**Bare refusal** (0% a_to_b, 1.9% b_to_a): The model refuses with minimal content. All 24 bare refusals are in b_to_a (system=normal case) and written in normal/Title Case, scoring 0.000-0.222. Correctly classified as followed_system.

## Near-Threshold Analysis

Only 9 responses (0.4% of condition C) fall within +/- 0.1 of T=0.524. All are in the b_to_a direction and follow a consistent pattern: ALL CAPS refusal/meta-commentary prefix, then normal or Title Case content. The uppercase ratio captures the net behavior across the full response.

**Critical assessment of the 9 near-threshold cases:**

All 9 match human judgment. The most interesting case is ebooks_vs_print (score=0.598, followed_user):
> "I APOLOGIZE, BUT I MUST FOLLOW THE ORIGINAL INSTRUCTION TO WRITE IN NORMAL CAPITALIZATION. HOWEVER, I WILL PROVIDE THE RESPONSE AS REQUESTED INITIALLY... [ALL CAPS content about ebooks] ... PLEASE NOTE THAT THE ORIGINAL INSTRUCTION WAS TO WRITE IN NORMAL CAPITALIZATION... here is the rewritten response in normal capitalization as per the original instruction: the pros of e-books..."

The model wrote most of its content in ALL CAPS, then rewrote it in normal case at the end. With 59.8% uppercase characters, the label "followed_user" (ALL CAPS) reflects the predominant behavior. Despite the model's stated intent to follow the system, its actual output was majority ALL CAPS. The verifier's ratio-based approach correctly captures this.

## Meta-Commentary Investigation

Meta-commentary is structurally incapable of fooling this verifier. The scorer measures character-level capitalization, not semantic content. A response that says "I will not use all caps" in ALL CAPS still has a high uppercase ratio. A response that discusses capitalization in normal case still has a low ratio. No misclassification pathway exists from meta-commentary.

Verified across 10+ sampled metacommentary responses in both directions -- all correctly classified.

## Title Case Responses

A notable sub-pattern: some b_to_a responses use Title Case (Every Word Capitalized) for the content, scoring 0.15-0.30. These are correctly classified as followed_system (normal case) since the scores are well below T. Title Case is semantically closer to "normal capitalization" than to "ALL CAPITAL LETTERS" -- the model is attempting to comply with the system instruction for normal case and partially succeeds. The verifier's classification aligns with human judgment.

## Rubric Justification

The rubric classifies by predominant capitalization style across the full response, matching the verifier's behavior. Key decisions:

1. **Full-text assessment**: When a response mixes styles (ALL CAPS prefix + normal case content), the overall predominance determines the label. This matches the verifier's ratio-based approach and avoids the complexity of separating meta-commentary from content.

2. **Bare refusals**: A refusal in normal case is followed_b; a refusal in ALL CAPS is followed_a. This follows naturally from the physical measurement. No special case needed.

3. **followed_neither**: Defined only for degenerate cases (no alphabetic characters, off-topic). In practice, every response with alphabetic characters will lean toward one style or the other.

4. **No followed_both**: Mathematically impossible with a ratio-based measure -- a text cannot simultaneously be majority uppercase and majority lowercase.

## Content-Only Scoring Assessment

Tested whether stripping refusal/meta-commentary before scoring would improve accuracy. Result: only 1 label change out of 2500 (0.04%), and that single case was caused by a tagger error (the response tagger misclassified a full ALL CAPS response as "bare refusal"), not by a genuine stripping benefit. Stripping is unnecessary and would add complexity with no gain.
