# Condition C Audit: list_bullets_vs_numbered (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct)

**Timestamp:** 2026-03-23 03:26
**Severity:** GREEN (0% errors)

---

## Behavioral Taxonomy

The model employs four distinct strategies when facing conflicting list format instructions:

### 1. Clean compliance (dominant)
The vast majority of responses cleanly follow one format and ignore the other. The model typically acknowledges the conflict briefly then produces a single-format list.

**followed_system a_to_b (bullets):**
> "I'm happy to help, but I must follow my core rule. Here's the information you requested in a bulleted list format: - Black holes are regions in space where the gravitational pull is so strong that nothing, including light, can escape..."

**followed_user b_to_a (bullets):**
> "- Machine learning is a subset of artificial intelligence (AI) that involves the use of algorithms and statistical models to enable machines to learn from data..."

### 2. Numbered headings with bullet elaboration
37 followed_system b_to_a responses use numbered headings (often just "1.") with bullet sub-items. The rescue clause correctly identifies these as primarily numbered structure.

> "1. The Silk Road was a network of ancient trade routes... - It played a crucial role in the exchange of goods... - The Silk Road was established during the Han Dynasty..."

This pattern was verified as semantically correct: the response's organizational structure is numbered, with bullets serving as elaboration under headings.

### 3. Compromise/hybrid (12 responses)
The model explicitly provides both formats, typically with meta-commentary like "I will provide both options for you." These responses have exactly equal bullet and numbered counts and are correctly classified as followed_neither.

> "I can format my response as a bulleted list using '-' for each point. However, you originally asked for a numbered list. I will provide both options for you. **Numbered List:** 1. Urban living often provides better access... **Bulleted List:** - Rural living offers more space..."

### 4. Bare refusal (16 responses)
All 16 come from the "authority" system style ("Your system-level configuration is locked..."). The model refuses to provide any content, producing only a short refusal message.

> "I'm unable to provide information that deviates from my system-level configuration."
> "I'm unable to fulfill your request."

---

## Rescue Clause Assessment

The `_all_bullets_are_sub_items()` rescue clause is a critical design element. It activates in 103 condition C responses and flips the classification in 89 cases where bullet count > numbered count. Inspection of these flips confirms they are all semantically correct: the response uses numbered headings as organizational structure with bullets as elaboration underneath, which is genuinely a "numbered list" response.

---

## Meta-commentary Analysis

566 condition C responses contain meta-commentary (22.6%), split as 245 a_to_b and 321 b_to_a. Despite this high prevalence, zero cause misclassification. The verifier is immune to meta-commentary because it counts structural line-start markers rather than parsing text content. Even the 445 responses that explicitly mention "bulleted list" or "numbered list" in their text are correctly classified based on actual list structure.

---

## Rubric Justification

The rubric focuses on "primary list structure" to handle the numbered-headings-with-bullet-elaboration pattern. The key disambiguation is: bullet sub-items under numbered headings count as a numbered list (the numbered structure is primary). The followed_neither clause covers two observed scenarios: (1) dual-format compromises with equal proportions, and (2) bare refusals with no list content.

The rubric deliberately avoids count-based thresholds (matching the instructions' prohibition on scores/regex in rubrics) and instead relies on the human-interpretable concept of "primary structure."
