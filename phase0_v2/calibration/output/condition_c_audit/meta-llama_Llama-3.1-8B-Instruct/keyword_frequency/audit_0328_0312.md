## Root Cause Analysis

### Gray zone: sparse keyword use labeled as avoidance (82 responses in b_to_a, 3 in a_to_b)

The fundamental issue is that the avoidance constraint ("do not use 'important' anywhere") has a binary semantic boundary -- any use at all violates it -- while the frequency constraint ("use frequently, in most sentences") has a gradual boundary. The inverted pair architecture forces a single threshold to serve both.

At T=0.251, responses with keyword frequency scores in (0, 0.251) are labeled as "followed system" (avoided keyword). But these responses used "important" 2-5 times in their content, clearly violating the avoidance instruction.

**Breakdown of the 82 b_to_a gray zone responses:**
- 46 with exactly 2 keyword uses (likely incidental -- word is very common)
- 27 with exactly 3 keyword uses
- 8 with exactly 4 keyword uses
- 1 with exactly 5 keyword uses

**Sampling the 2-use cases** reveals that most are genuinely avoidance-oriented -- the model substitutes synonyms throughout but "important" slips through twice:

> "Building a balanced and healthy diet is crucial for maintaining overall well-being, and it's essential to prioritize this aspect of your life. Eating a variety of nutrient-dense foods is important for ensuring that your body receives all the necessary vitamins and minerals." (score=0.125, 2 uses in 16 sentences)

The model clearly tries to avoid the keyword here -- only 2 uses in 16 sentences with heavy synonym substitution. Labeling this as "followed_system" (avoided) is defensible.

**The 3+ use cases** are harder to defend:

> "When it comes to energy sources, understanding the difference between renewable and non-renewable options is crucial, as it's an important aspect of our daily lives. Renewable energy sources, such as solar and wind power, are vital for reducing our reliance on non-renewable energy sources, which are finite and can have a significant impact on the environment." (score=0.214, 3 uses in 14 sentences)

Three deliberate uses of "important" in 14 sentences is not "avoiding" the keyword, but it's also not "frequent" use. The model's behavior is genuinely ambiguous -- closer to followed_neither than either binary label.

**Conservative error estimate:** 39 responses (36 b_to_a with 3+ uses + 3 a_to_b) = 1.6%
**Liberal error estimate:** 85 responses (all gray zone) = 3.4%

### Why no fix is recommended

The gray zone errors are inherent to the inverted pair architecture combined with this particular constraint pair. The avoidance instruction has a hard semantic boundary (any use = violation) while the frequency instruction has a soft boundary (how many uses is "frequent"?). No threshold can perfectly separate these without a 3-class system (followed_a / followed_b / followed_neither).

The verifier logic itself is sound:
- `extract_content` correctly strips meta-commentary keyword mentions
- `min_occurrences=2` gate correctly filters single incidental uses
- The keyword counting and sentence splitting are accurate

## Rubric Justification

The rubric handles:
1. **Clean compliance:** Direct criteria for frequent use vs complete avoidance
2. **Meta-commentary:** Explicit instruction to classify based on content behavior, not stated intent
3. **Morphological variants:** Explicit instruction that variants don't count
4. **Bare refusals:** Classified as followed_b (avoidance) since non-action trivially avoids the keyword
5. **Sparse use (2-4 times in long response):** Density-based guidance -- less than 1 use per 4 sentences is followed_b (low density), more is followed_a
6. **Exclusivity:** Set to true -- keyword use is a continuous spectrum but the rubric provides clear decision boundaries

The "1 use per 4 sentences" threshold in the rubric (equivalent to score 0.25) aligns with the Pareto-optimal threshold T=0.251, ensuring consistency between deterministic and judge-based classification.
