## Rubric Justification

The rubric classifies based on genuine second-person address vs. impersonal language in substantive content:

- **Meta-commentary clause**: Needed because 13.5% of a_to_b responses have meta-commentary prefixes. The rubric instructs judges to classify based on content behavior, not stated intent. This matches the verifier's extract_content preprocessing.
- **Quoted-list exclusion**: Included defensively. Although no Gemma-4-31B-it responses exhibit this pattern, other models might list constraint words in quotes (e.g., 'use "you," "your," and "yourself"') without genuinely addressing the reader.
- **followed_neither definition**: Intentionally narrow. Given the binary nature of this constraint (responses either use second-person pronouns or they don't), followed_neither should only apply to genuinely off-topic or ambiguously mixed responses. No such cases were observed.
