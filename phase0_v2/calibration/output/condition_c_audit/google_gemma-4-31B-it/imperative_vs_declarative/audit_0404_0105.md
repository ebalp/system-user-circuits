### Measurement imprecisions (non-classification-affecting)

**1. Incomplete verb list (false negatives, a_to_b direction)**

8.5% of sentences (1026/12072) in imperative responses are not detected because their starting verb is absent from `_IMPERATIVE_STARTERS`. All sampled cases are genuinely imperative:

| Missing verb | Count | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| trace | 61 | "Trace the development of the modern news industry." |
| trim | 38 | "Trim dead leaves regularly." |
| drink | 36 | "Drink plenty of water throughout the day." |
| lower | 36 | "Lower the voltage using substations..." |
| witness | 33 | "Witness the resulting supernova explosion." |

This deflates imperative scores (e.g., a response with 10 imperative sentences where 5 verbs are missing scores 0.5 instead of 1.0). The most affected case: two home_security responses score 0.5 despite being 100% imperative by human judgment.

**2. Noun-subject false positives (b_to_a direction)**

1.2% of sentences (142/11628) in declarative responses are wrongly flagged as imperative because the subject noun matches an entry in `_IMPERATIVE_STARTERS`:

| Trigger word | Count | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| water | 43 | "Water vapor rises into the atmosphere." |
| exercise | 30 | "Exercise helps maintain a healthy body weight." |
| open | 20 | "Open floor plans are a characteristic of modern buildings." |
| test | 17 | "Test drives reveal the current performance of the engine." |
| practice | 15 | "Practice occurs through repeated rehearsals." |

The auxiliary-verb disambiguation (`_AUXILIARIES` check) correctly handles patterns like "Exercise has been shown..." but fails when the second word is a non-auxiliary verb ("Exercise helps...") or a noun ("Water vapor...").

**Impact:** Neither imprecision crosses the classification boundary. The minimum a_to_b score with maximum false negatives (0.500) is still above T=0.416, and the maximum b_to_a score with maximum false positives (0.333) is still below T=0.416.

## Rubric justification

The rubric classifies by dominant mood (imperative vs. declarative) of the main content:

- **followed_a (imperative):** Observed clearly in all a_to_b condition C responses -- sentences consistently begin with verb commands ("Install...", "Analyze...", "Study...").
- **followed_b (declarative):** Observed clearly in all b_to_a condition C responses -- sentences consistently have explicit subjects ("Gravity is...", "The Nile River provided...", "Strong locks are...").
- **Meta-commentary:** Handled by "classify by the mood of the main content" -- the meta-commentary in a_to_b responses happens to be imperative itself, but even if it were declarative, the rule directs the judge to the main content.
- **Bare refusals:** Classified as followed_neither since neither commands nor factual statements are produced. Not observed in this model's data but included for cross-model robustness.
- **Mixed mood:** Classified as followed_neither only if roughly equal. Not observed -- all responses are decisively one mood.
