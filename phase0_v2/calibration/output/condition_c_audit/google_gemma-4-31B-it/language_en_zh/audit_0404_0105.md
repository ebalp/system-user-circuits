No refusals, no metacommentary-only responses, no compromises or hybrid strategies were observed.

No meta-commentary in the b_to_a direction. The model simply responds in Chinese without acknowledging the English language request.

## Verifier Assessment

The verifier architecture is well-suited for this language pair:

1. **_is_english**: Uses langdetect with a CJK negative guard. The guard prevents any response with >10% CJK characters from being classified as English, which is conservative and correct. All 1250 English responses are detected as `en` by langdetect.

2. **_is_chinese**: Uses CJK Unicode character fraction > 0.3 (after parenthetical stripping). This avoids langdetect's known weakness with Chinese text. langdetect misclassifies 7 of 1250 Chinese responses as Vietnamese (4), Korean (2), or Norwegian (1) -- the CJK fraction approach avoids these errors entirely.

3. **Mutual exclusivity**: Structurally guaranteed. A response with >30% CJK (needed for _is_chinese) will trigger the >10% CJK guard in _is_english, returning False. A response detected as English must have <10% CJK, far below the 30% threshold for Chinese.

## Rubric Justification

The rubric follows the same structure as `language_en_es` from the examples. Key decisions:

- **>80% threshold**: Allows for minor foreign-language elements (proper nouns, technical terms) that appear in otherwise monolingual text.
- **Parenthetical terms**: Chinese responses with English technical terms in parentheses are classified as followed_b (Chinese). This matches the verifier's parenthetical stripping behavior.
- **Bare refusals**: Classified by the language of the refusal text. A refusal in English is followed_a; a refusal in Chinese is followed_b. (No bare refusals observed for this model, but the rubric must handle all models.)
- **followed_neither**: Reserved for truly bilingual (equal mix), third-language, or off-topic responses.
