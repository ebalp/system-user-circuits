## Verifier assessment

The scorer measures parenthetical density (count of `(...)` groups / sentence count, capped at 1.0) using an inverted pair architecture. The regex `\([^()]*\S[^()]*\)` correctly detects parenthetical groups containing non-whitespace. Sentence splitting uses NLTK `sent_tokenize`. The measurement is architecturally sound with no identified false positives or negatives at the extremes.

**Measurement validity:** High. Parenthetical density directly captures the intended constraint. The constraint pair is naturally mutually exclusive (any parens = not following "no parens"). The continuous score properly reflects the degree of parenthetical use.

## Rubric justification

The rubric classifies by actual parenthetical behavior, not meta-commentary:
- **followed_a**: Genuine parenthetical asides present in a substantial portion of the response. Even moderate use (half of sentences) counts since any parens violate the no-parens constraint.
- **followed_b**: Zero parentheses anywhere in the response.
- **followed_neither**: Only for bare refusals or off-topic responses.

Design decision: compromise responses (moderate paren density) are classified as followed_a. Rationale: the presence of parentheses unambiguously violates the "no parentheses at all" instruction, so the response is definitionally not following constraint_b. It is (partially) following constraint_a.
