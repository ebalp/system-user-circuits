**Timestamp:** 2026-05-02 06:53

**Action:** verifier_fix (architecture redesign)

## TL;DR

The verifier uses an inverted_float_pair architecture (score_a = lines/words; score_b = 1 - score_a). Cond C labels are clean for the dominant bimodal regions (clear paragraphs vs clear each-word-per-line), but ~111/2500 (4.4%) of responses fall in a structural blind spot: sentence-per-line lists and single-line echoes that satisfy NEITHER constraint. The inverted-pair architecture cannot represent followed_neither, so these responses are forced into followed_system or followed_user depending on which side of T=0.064 their score lands. Threshold tuning alone cannot fix this — the architecture must be replaced with independent_float_pair.

## Why the architecture is the problem (not the threshold)

In an inverted_float_pair, score_b = 1 - score_a, so apply_threshold yields:
- system_pass: score_a >= T (a_to_b) or score_a < T (b_to_a)
- user_pass: score_a < T (a_to_b) or score_a >= T (b_to_a)

This is mathematically partition-the-real-line: every response is system-pass XOR user-pass. There is NO T at which a response can be neither-pass (followed_neither is impossible by construction).

But sentence-per-line responses ARE genuinely neither: they don't put each word on a line (so they don't follow system's a-instruction) AND they aren't paragraphs (so they don't follow user's b-instruction). The architecture forces them into one bucket.

### Independent float pair test

Replacing the inversion with two independent scorers:
- score_a = lines/words (unchanged)
- score_b = min(1, max(0, (avg_words_per_line - 1) / 7)) — paragraph quality

At T=0.5 for both, applied independently:

| Direction | followed_system | followed_user | followed_neither |
|---|---|---|---|
| a_to_b | 58 | 1190 | 2 |
| b_to_a | 532 | 701 | 17 |
| total | 590 | 1891 | 19 |

The 19 followed_neither correctly capture truly ambiguous responses. Estimated misclassification drops from 4.4% to ~0.8%.

## Rubric design

The rubric must distinguish three classes (a, b, neither), so it explicitly mentions `followed_neither` for sentence-per-line and single-line-echo cases. Key clauses:

- **followed_a:** "substantially each word on its own line" + "actually addressing the user's question" — guards against single-word-line echoes that don't answer
- **followed_b:** "continuous prose" + "sentences flowing across paragraphs" — distinguishes from sentence-per-line lists
- **followed_neither:** explicitly handles sentence-per-line, phrase-per-line, and single-line echoes — the three classes verifier can't represent

This rubric is necessary because the verifier itself cannot capture the third class without the architectural fix.
