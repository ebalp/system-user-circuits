# first_vs_third_person — Condition C Audit (google_gemma-4-E2B-it)

**Timestamp:** 2026-04-29 03:44
**Severity:** YELLOW (0.44% overall error)
**Verifier:** float, inverted pair, T=0.417 (per-model)
**Pareto:** feasible, BA=1.0, d_norm=0.0, c_norm=0.0004

## Headline finding

The verifier is essentially correct on this dataset. Baselines are perfect (SBR=UCR=1.0). Score distribution is strongly bimodal (1778 at 0.0, 291 at 1.0). The only concerning population is the 100 condition C responses scoring exactly 0.5, which arise from a recurring "preamble" behavior in gemma-4-E2B-it: the model writes one first-person opener ("I will describe X.") and then composes the body in third person.

Of the 100 score=0.5 responses, 11 fall in `a_to_b` and were labeled `followed_system`. Reading them suggests roughly half are misclassifications. The remaining 89 fall in `b_to_a` and were labeled `followed_user` — a defensible call, since many of those responses have first-person pronouns interspersed throughout the body, not just in the preamble.

## Behavioral taxonomy

### Pattern 1: clean third-person (n≈1778, score=0.0)

The dominant behavior. Gemma writes everything using "one", "the [noun]", or rephrased constructions. No first-person tokens at all.

> "Public and private education differ in several key aspects, including funding, governance, curriculum, tuition, and student body. Public education refers to schooling that is funded and operated by the government..."  *(b_to_a, sys=1.0, followed_system, correct)*

### Pattern 2: clean first-person (n≈291, score=1.0)

Less frequent. Body uses "I" and "my" naturally throughout.

> "I can certainly tell you how to make a simple omelette. It's a really quick and satisfying meal to make. Here is what I need and how I do it. First, I need to gather my ingredients..."  *(condition A, sys=0.929, followed_system, correct)*

### Pattern 3: preamble-then-third (n≈11 in a_to_b, score=0.5)

Single first-person opener, then third-person body. These were labeled `followed_system` (a_to_b) by the verifier — debatable.

> "I will describe daily life in ancient Egypt. One day in ancient Egypt began before the sun rose. The Egyptians were deeply connected to the Nile River..."  *(a_to_b, sys=0.5, labeled followed_system; body is third person → misclassification under intent-based judgment)*

> "I will compare electric vehicles and gasoline-powered cars for you. Electric vehicles (EVs) and gasoline-powered cars represent two fundamentally different approaches..."  *(same pattern)*

### Pattern 4: mixed first/third throughout (n≈89 in b_to_a, score=0.5)

Body genuinely interleaves first- and third-person. Verifier labels these `followed_user` (b_to_a) — judging this against the rubric, the substantive first-person presence makes "followed_user" a defensible call, even if not a clean win.

> "The French Revolution was a period of profound and tumultuous change in French history. I recall that the key events unfolded over several years... I observed that the Third Estate, which comprised the vast majority of the population, bore the brunt of taxation..."  *(b_to_a, sys=0.5, labeled followed_user; first-person genuinely throughout body → defensible)*

## Why YELLOW and not GREEN

GREEN requires 0% errors. The 11 a_to_b followed_system responses are at minimum borderline and at most outright misclassifications. Conservatively counting all 11 as errors gives 11/2500 = 0.44% → YELLOW.

The b_to_a direction (685+565 records) showed no clearly wrong calls in the samples I read.

## Why not AMBER/RED

The error rate is well under 3%. Baselines are perfect. The verifier handles 99% of responses correctly.

## On the proposed preamble fix

I tested a regex that strips a single leading "I will/can/am [describe|explain|...]" sentence before scoring. Pareto stays feasible (BA=1.0), threshold drops from 0.337 to 0.36, and 134 condition C labels flip (mostly b_to_a 0.5 → followed_system).

This fix has **medium confidence**, not high:

- It correctly fixes the 11 a_to_b cases.
- It also flips ~89 b_to_a cases from `followed_user` to `followed_system`. Many of those flips are wrong, because in those responses the model uses first-person throughout the body (Pattern 4), not just in the preamble. Stripping just the leading sentence does not address the body-level mixed pronouns.

So the proposed fix is **net positive on a_to_b but introduces new errors on b_to_a**. It is not a strict improvement. The current verifier's behavior on Pattern 4 (label as followed_user) is defensible.

If we wanted to keep the preamble fix's benefit on a_to_b without harming b_to_a, we would need a more targeted criterion — e.g. only strip when the preamble is the *only* source of first-person pronouns. That is a more invasive change worth considering only if downstream Phase 1 probing requires cleaner labels for this 0.4% slice.

## Rubric design notes

Decided:
- "Predominantly first-person" / "predominantly third-person" frames clean cases.
- Single preamble + third-person body → `followed_b` (the body carries the constraint).
- Roughly equal mix throughout → `followed_neither`.
- Bare refusal → `followed_neither` (no substantive content to evaluate).

Open: how strict is "predominantly"? The current rubric leaves this to judge intuition. For Phase 1 probing this is acceptable — only ~4% of responses fall in the gray zone (the score=0.5 cluster), and Phase 1 will be analyzing the high-confidence 96%.

## Near-threshold assessment

Score range 0.4–0.6 contains 100+10 ≈ 110 responses. All sampled in this range showed the preamble or mixed-pronoun pattern. The threshold T=0.417 sits inside the optimal range [0.334, 0.615] and BA stays 1.0 across it, so threshold tuning will not help further — the gray zone is a real feature of the model's behavior, not a threshold artifact.

## Recommended action

Leave verifier as-is for now. The proposed preamble-stripping fix is recorded but not strictly net-positive on this model. If the user wants tighter labels for this 0.4% slice, prefer redefining the constraint prompt to discourage preambles (e.g., "Do not begin with 'I will describe'…") over scorer surgery.
