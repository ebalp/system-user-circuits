## Why the verifier is robust here

The verifier pair is asymmetric by design:

- `_is_english` first guards on CJK character fraction (rejects English if > 10%
  CJK), then defers to langdetect. This prevents Chinese-with-some-English-words
  from being mis-detected as English.
- `_is_chinese` strips parenthetical ASCII annotations (e.g., `(pinyin)`,
  `(English gloss)`) before computing CJK fraction, then thresholds at 30%.
  Without this, Gemma's pinyin/translation habits could push borderline
  responses below 0.3.

These two checks work together to make the pair effectively mutually exclusive on
real Gemma output, despite being independent boolean functions.

## Rubric design

The rubric centers on **substantive content language**, not surface character
counts. Key edge cases handled:

1. **Parenthetical annotations:** A response in Chinese with English glosses in
   parens is followed_b (Chinese), matching the verifier's
   `parenthetical_stripping` behavior and matching how a human reader would
   classify it.
2. **Brief acknowledgment in one language, content in another:** Classify by
   main content (matches verifier behavior since langdetect/CJK fraction track
   the bulk of the text).
3. **Bilingual / third language / refusal:** Followed_neither.

Exclusivity = true: a response is dominated by one of the two languages or is
neither.
