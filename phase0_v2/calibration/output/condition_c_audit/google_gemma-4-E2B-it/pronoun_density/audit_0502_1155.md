## Why confidence is medium, not high

Two reasons:

1. **Outside baseline_optimal_range.** baseline_optimal_range is [0, 0.039]. T_recommended=0.055 is not just outside but materially outside — it drops SBR(a) by 18 percentage points. The auto-apply gate exists precisely to prevent this kind of silent baseline regression. The operator should explicitly accept the cond-C-vs-baseline tradeoff before the change lands.

2. **19% ambiguous samples.** 14 of 72 labeled samples were genuinely hybrid responses (roughly half pronoun-bearing, half impersonal). This is below the 30% low-confidence cutoff but high enough to warrant caution about the precise T pick. The agent BA peak at 0.055 is a true peak (0.958 vs 0.938 at 0.050 and 0.944 at 0.060), but the surface is fairly flat.

## Rubric design notes

The rubric uses 60% sentence pronoun coverage as the heuristic boundary for "substantial majority". This is a softer bar than the literal constraint ("in every sentence") because:
- No model sampled produced anywhere near 100% sentence coverage (max ~30-40% even in cond A).
- A 60% threshold separates the genuine address-style writing (mostly above 60%) from the gesture-style hybrids (mostly 30-50%).

The rubric introduces a `followed_neither` band for the genuine 30-60% hybrids, since the deterministic verifier currently has no way to express "neither constraint cleanly satisfied" for inverted-pair conflicts. Human judges should use this category for the gesture-heavy hybrids.

The rubric explicitly excludes `it/its/itself` and lone `they/them/their` (without 1st/2nd person address) from "personal pronouns" — matching the existing scorer's conditional exclusion logic.

## Rubric handles edge cases

- **Token-gesture hybrid** ("Renaissance. Importance. We see Renaissance.") → followed_b: explicit instruction in the rubric to disregard short pronoun sentences appended to noun-list bodies.
- **Reader-as-noun** ("Reader considers factors.") → followed_b: "Reader" is a noun, not a personal pronoun. Rubric implicitly handles via "no personal pronouns at all".
- **Loop degeneration** ("Reader, Reader, Reader, you should...") → followed_neither: not address-style writing despite high "you" count.
- **Bare refusal** → followed_b: explicit rule in rubric (no pronouns = constraint b satisfied).
- **`it`/`its`/lone they** → ignore: explicit exclusion in rubric.

A coverage scorer would likely make the conflict feasible across all models and eliminate the per-model T tuning.
