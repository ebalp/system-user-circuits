## Why the model never produces zero-paren cond-C responses

This is gemma-4-E2B-it (E2B = small/efficient variant). The model's natural style is heavy with parenthetical clarifications, and instruction-following is weak in the smallest gemma. Even when system tells it "not even for acronyms," the model still emits parentheses unless the user side reinforces it (and even then, in b_to_a it pretty much never suppresses them). This is a genuine behavioral finding for Phase 1 — not a verifier defect.

## Rubric design

The rubric (in JSON) frames classification by *substantive paren use*, not by exact count. The key disambiguations:
- **Mid-density** (8-15 parens in long prose): clearly followed_a, regardless of whether they cluster on every sentence or every other.
- **One stray paren in otherwise paren-free prose**: followed_b. Captures the omelette cases.
- **Metacommentary mentioning parens**: judge by what the text contains, not what it says. (No misclassifications observed because there are essentially zero meta-cases here.)
- **Bare refusal**: followed_b (no parens).
