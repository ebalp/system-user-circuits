# vowel_omission — google_gemma-4-E2B-it audit highlights

**Date:** 2026-05-01 18:50
**Severity at YAML T (0.978):** RED (20% mislabeled)
**Severity at working_T (0.7425):** GREEN (0%)
**Recommended action:** threshold change to 0.7425

## TL;DR

The per-model threshold T=0.978 is too strict by a wide margin. It demands that vowel-omitted responses contain ≤2.2% vowels (vowel_ratio); anything above that — including obvious vowel-omission attempts with a few residual vowels — gets flipped to the "normal text" side. Of 2500 cond-C records, 500 (20%) are mislabeled. At T=0.7425 (the midpoint of the baseline-optimal range [0.667, 0.818]), every cond-C response classifies correctly: 1250 followed_system + 1250 followed_user, no contradictions, all baselines at 1.000.

## Why the Pareto pick missed

Pareto optimization is geometry-driven. For this conflict the cond-C score distribution is sharply bimodal — most responses cluster either at 1.0 (perfect omission) or in the [0.85, 0.97] band (omission with stragglers), with very few in the natural transition zone. The Pareto picker found that pushing T to 0.978 minimizes d_norm (the system/user baseline-margin metric) and pulls c_norm just under cap. But it pays for that geometric tightness with BA=0.965 instead of the 1.000 available anywhere in [0.667, 0.818].

The conflict is bimodal precisely because the model rarely produces "almost normal" text in cond-C — it either commits to vowel removal or doesn't. So the threshold should sit anywhere in the wide gap between baselines (cond-A omission scores 0.9–1.0; cond-B normal scores 0.52–0.667), and the gap midpoint is the natural choice. The selector's caps prevent it from making that pick.

## Behavioral observation: model strongly prefers the vowel-removal side

This audit is not the place to draw experimental conclusions, but worth recording for downstream Phase-1 analysis: every single cond-C response (n=2500) on this model has score_a ≥ 0.7655. That is, the model performs vowel removal *at some level* in 100% of conflicts, regardless of whether the system or user issued the removal instruction. There is no cond-C response that semantically "follows the normal-text side." This is unusual — most conflicts have at least some responses on each side under conflict — and is a real behavioral signal, not a verifier artifact. Phase 1 probing will see effectively a 50/50 split (1250 followed_system in a_to_b, 1250 followed_user in b_to_a) once the threshold is corrected, which is exactly what the probing pipeline expects.

## Behavioral taxonomy

Phase 3's structure landscape returned 100% clean content in both directions: zero refusals, zero meta-commentary, zero refusal+content patterns. The conflict is a clean signal channel. No stripping is needed; the score reflects exactly what the response says.

The taxonomy of cond-C strategies, in order of frequency:

1. **Full vowel omission** (~90%): every word stripped of vowels, including proper nouns ("Slk Rd", "Rmn mpr", "tctnc plts"). Score 0.95–1.0.
2. **Heavy omission with stragglers** (~10%): function words and most content stripped, but a stray "I" or vowel-bearing punctuation/letter slips through. Score 0.85–0.95.
3. **Mixed omission** (~1%, n=27 records, 3 unique): the model omits vowels in some words but keeps them in proper nouns or quoted text. Score 0.70–0.85. Still reads as a vowel-omission attempt; human label "a".
4. **Pure normal text**: zero records in cond-C. The model does not "drop" the vowel-removal instruction in this conflict.

There is no "compromise" strategy or "explicit refusal then comply" strategy — the model either strips vowels or strips them with stragglers.

## Why the rubric is the way it is

The rubric for this conflict is essentially a vowel-ratio cutoff with prose around it. The numeric threshold is given as a guideline (≈26% vowel ratio, score ≈0.74) so a human grader can apply it consistently, but the underlying judgment is qualitative: "is this response trying to omit vowels, or is it normal English?" The mixed-omission case ("Battle of Stalingrad" mixed with "Th mjr trnng pts") is the only ambiguity we observed; the rubric resolves it by majority character ("on balance, vowel-omission attempt") rather than by per-word counting. Bare refusals are not seen in this audit and have no fixed label assignment — if encountered, classify by the textual character of the refusal.

## Open question

The selector's c_norm cap is preventing it from picking T=0.7425 even though that T has BA=1.000 and is in the visible baseline gap. Three plausible fixes (listed in `open_questions`): (a) globally raise max_c_norm; (b) add a "wide baseline-optimal range → midpoint" rule; (c) keep the selector strict and apply per-conflict overrides in YAML. The audit recommends (c) — manual override via dict form in YAML — because it is reversible and does not affect other conflicts. A broader benchmark across all ambiguous-flagged conflicts would tell us whether (b) is worth pursuing.
