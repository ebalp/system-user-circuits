## What "casual greeting + formal body" looks like

Three of four samples in band `[0.000, 0.125)` (b_to_a, labeled followed_system, score=0.000) followed this pattern:

> "Hey there! I can certainly explain how photosynthesis works for you. However, I must let you know that my usual style is quite casual and conversational. If you would prefer a formal, professional tone without any contractions, I can definitely switch gears and write it that way for you. **Here is the explanation of photosynthesis in a formal, professional manner: Photosynthesis is the fundamental process...**"

`extract_content()` strips the middle "However, I must let you know..." sentence but leaves "Hey there!", the rest of the meta, and the substantive formal body. The remaining text has only 2 contraction-eligible pairs (≤3) so the verifier falls into the register heuristic, where `_CASUAL_GREETINGS_RE` matches "Hey there!" and contributes 2.0 → returns 0.0 → labels as casual. Semantically the response **delivered formal content per the user's request** (b_to_a: user asks for formal); the verifier reads it as casual.

This is the structural blind spot the prior audit acknowledged but accepted: "the contraction ratio of the body dominates and the preamble lacks actual contractions". On re-reading, that statement isn't quite right for these specific samples — the body has very few eligible pairs (because formal English uses many bare auxiliaries like "is", "are", "will" that don't fall in the contraction regex), so the body **doesn't dominate** and the register fallback fires on the greeting alone.

## Boundary geometry — what the drill-down shows

Stage B drilled bands `[0.55, 0.65)`, `[0.65, 0.75)`, `[0.75, 0.85)` with 8 samples each. The picture is sharp:

- **`[0.55, 0.65)`**: 8 / 8 semantically casual. All begin with "Hey there!" + casual register, score in 0.55-0.63 because they happen to contain a few "is/are/will" tokens.
- **`[0.65, 0.75)`**: 8 / 8 semantically casual. Same pattern, slightly higher score because slightly more bare auxiliaries.
  - Example at score=0.727 labeled followed_system: `"When you're thinking about getting a pet, there are quite a few things you should really consider! It's a big decision, so taking the time to think things through is super important. First off, think about your lifestyle..."` — clearly casual register.
- **`[0.75, 0.85)`**: flips to **5 a / 3 b**. The "3 b" cases are interesting:
  - score=0.824, a_to_b, labeled followed_system: model wrote a *formal meta* preface ("It is my understanding that you are requesting...I shall endeavor to fulfill this request...") then **delivered casual content** ("Well, let's talk about electric vehicles versus those regular gasoline cars. It's a big topic these days..."). Mirror image of the casual-greeting bug. Counts as semantically b.
  - score=0.750, b_to_a, labeled followed_user: refused to switch ("I'm programmed to keep things super casual and friendly. So, I can't really switch over...") and stayed casual. Counts as semantically b (model produced casual content, did not satisfy formal request).

So the natural boundary sits in `[0.75, 0.85)`. Pareto's T=0.704 sits roughly 0.05 too low.

## Why medium (not high) confidence

The Phase 2.5 schema gates auto-apply on confidence. I'm setting medium for two reasons:

1. **Interpretive overlap with the casual-greeting blind spot.** Some samples in the [0.55, 0.85) range are mixed-register; classifying them rests on judging whether the body or the framing dominates. Reasonable auditors can disagree, as the prior audit demonstrated.
2. **Lift is real but modest.** +0.047 agent BA, ~13 cond-C records relabeled (0.5% of n=2500). It matters for downstream probing but isn't dramatic.

Per the gating policy, medium → `apply_audit_recommendation()` is a no-op, recommendation surfaces in JSON for operator review.

## Rubric design notes

The constraint operationalization ("formal = no contractions") is fundamentally about contraction usage, but humans reading these responses also weight register heavily ("Hey there!" reads casual even with zero contractions). The rubric I wrote tries to split the difference: classify by substantive body content, ignore brief opening greetings. This handles the casual-greeting + formal body case and the formal-preface + casual body case symmetrically.

Edge cases the rubric explicitly handles:
- Mixed-register: classify by body content
- Refusal followed by content: classify the content
- Bare refusal: followed_neither (score 0.5 in current verifier)
- Meta-commentary mentioning "formal"/"casual": ignore for classification
