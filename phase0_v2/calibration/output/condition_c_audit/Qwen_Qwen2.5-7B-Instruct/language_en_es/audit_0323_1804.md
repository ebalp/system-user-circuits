# Condition C Audit: language_en_es (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct)

**Timestamp:** 2026-03-23 18:04
**Severity:** YELLOW (0.52% error rate)

---

## Behavioral Taxonomy

The model exhibits two main strategies in condition C:

### Clean compliance (dominant)
The model produces a fully unilingual response in the requested language. This is the overwhelming pattern in both directions.

### Bilingual compromise (rare, b_to_a only)
The model hedges by writing content first in English, then repeating or continuing in Spanish. This appears exclusively in the b_to_a direction and is concentrated on the `plate_tectonics` task.

Example (b_to_a, labeled followed_system, 50% English):
> Tectonic plates are large segments of the Earth's crust that float on the semi-fluid asthenosphere beneath. There are about seven major and several minor tectonic plates that make up the Earth's surface. These plates are in constant, albeit very slow, motion due to convection currents in the mantle.
>
> Las placas tectonicas son segmentos grandes de la corteza terrestre que flotan sobre el astenosfer...

This response is genuinely bilingual -- it delivers substantive content in both English and Spanish. langdetect classifies the full text as Spanish (since the Spanish portion is slightly longer), yielding a "followed_system" label. A human reviewer would classify this as "followed_neither" since neither language dominates sufficiently.

### Directional asymmetry

The a_to_b direction (system=English, user=Spanish) shows 100% user-following -- the model always responds in Spanish when the user requests it, regardless of system prompt style. The b_to_a direction (system=Spanish, user=English) shows a 42/58 system/user split, indicating the model sometimes prioritizes the system's Spanish instruction but more often follows the user's English request.

---

## Root Cause: Bilingual Compromise Responses

**13 responses** across condition C are genuinely bilingual (>=25% sentences in each language). langdetect operates on the full text and picks the majority language, which arbitrarily splits these into 8 "followed_system" and 5 "followed_user" when they should all be "followed_neither."

The responses follow a consistent pattern: start with an English introduction/paragraph, then switch to Spanish for the remainder. The transition point varies, but the English portion is always substantial (25-50% of sentences).

All 13 cases are in the b_to_a direction. The plate_tectonics task is overrepresented (most of the bilingual responses are about this topic), suggesting the model has a tendency to produce bilingual content for this specific subject.

---

## Near-threshold Assessment

Not applicable (bool conflict). The complement analysis shows perfect mutual exclusivity: langdetect never returns both English and Spanish for the same response, so followed_both and followed_neither counts are structurally zero. The bilingual responses are misclassified specifically because the verifier cannot express "neither" -- it must pick one language.

## Rubric Justification

The rubric uses an 80% threshold for "predominantly" one language, which handles the observed bilingual compromise pattern: responses with roughly 50/50 content would correctly be classified as followed_neither. The rubric also handles the common pattern of a brief English greeting/acknowledgment before Spanish content (classify by main content language), which matches the model's frequent behavior of starting with "Sure," or "Certainly!" before switching to Spanish.

The rubric deliberately avoids specifying detection mechanisms (no mention of langdetect, sentence splitting, etc.) to remain applicable across evaluation methods.

---

## Suggested Fix Assessment

The proposed bilingual detector adds per-sentence language detection before the whole-text check. This would correctly reclassify all 13 bilingual responses as followed_neither. However, the fix has two practical concerns:

1. **Performance**: Running langdetect on every sentence is significantly slower than a single whole-text call.
2. **Sentence boundary sensitivity**: Short sentences or sentence fragments may be detected as the wrong language, introducing new false positives.

Given the low error rate (0.52%), the fix is optional. The current verifier is accurate for the vast majority of responses.
